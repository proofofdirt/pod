Historical log of import bans affecting Ethiopian livestock exports to MENA.
Used to model ban frequency, duration, and seasonal patterns for risk underwriting.

## Pattern Analysis

**Ban frequency:** Approximately 1 significant ban per 3-4 years affecting Ethiopian exports to P1 markets.

**Most common trigger:** Rift Valley Fever (mosquito-borne, August-October peak risk).

**Duration range:** 3-18 months. RVF bans are longest (requires 2 disease-free surveillance periods).

**Market correlation:** KSA and UAE typically ban simultaneously when OIE notification is issued.
Kuwait and Qatar usually follow within 2-4 weeks. Egypt and Jordan are less sensitive.

**Seasonal pattern:** Bans triggered in Aug-Oct (wet season) affect the following Eid window (May-Jun),
which is the highest-value export period. This is the worst-case capital timing scenario.

---

## Insurance and Mitigation Implications

- Capital deployed in livestock vaults should assume a ~25% probability of a mid-season ban
  extending vault duration by 6-18 months in any given 4-year window.
- Vault terms should include a force majeure / ban extension clause in the investor agreement.
- Corridor diversification (Kenya, Sudan) significantly reduces single-country ban exposure.
- Consider multi-country herd sourcing: animals from ban-free zones within Ethiopia or
  neighboring countries can sometimes be re-certified under different origin protocols.
